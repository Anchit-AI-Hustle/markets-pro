## 2. Candidates considered but disqualified before scoring

Two ideas were ruled out immediately — they fail hard charter constraints
regardless of how well they'd otherwise score, so they were not carried into
the scoring table.

- **Physical D2C tea/wellness product brand.** This is the closest match to
  the operator's category knowledge, but it fails the model on structure, not
  ambition: inventory, packaging, and minimum order quantities eat most of
  the Rs 2–3L ceiling before a single unit ships; fulfillment is an ongoing
  operational load that doesn't fit "the work is done once"; and reaching
  Rs 1L/month at <30% CAC on a physical product against cold Meta/Google
  traffic, with zero brand trust, inside 8–12 weeks, is not realistic even
  for an operator who has done this well for someone else's brand. Ruled out
  on cash ceiling and timeline, not on skill fit.
- **Synchronous Klaviyo/Meta ads consulting retainer.** The single most
  obvious "sell what you know" move, and explicitly disqualified by the
  charter itself: retainers require client calls and ongoing service work,
  both banned. Any revenue here is capped by the operator's calendar, which
  is capped at 7 hours/week — the charter's exact "asset does the selling"
  test fails.

## 4. The pick

**Candidate A — Productized async growth diagnostic reports for D2C brand
founders.**

Weighted score 6.25/10, clearly ahead of the field (next-best is C at 4.95).

### Why

- **Unfair advantage is real and maximal (10/10).** This is not adjacent to
  the operator's background — it *is* the operator's background. Diagnosing
  what's broken in a D2C brand's Meta/Google accounts, Klaviyo flows, and
  landing pages is the exact job the operator held as Senior Director of
  Growth. A random smart founder would need months just to know what a good
  flow looks like.
- **Distribution is the honest weak point, scored at 5/10, not padded
  higher.** There is no owned channel. But this is the one candidate where
  "must invent a channel from zero" is least punishing: the buyer (D2C
  founders/marketers in the operator's former category) is a narrow,
  identifiable list the operator can build and target directly — cold
  outbound to a known ICP, not mass-market cold traffic. That's a real
  channel, just a manual, linearly-scaling one, which is why it isn't scored
  higher than 5.
- **Cash ceiling is comfortably met (9/10).** Landing page, Stripe, an
  intake form, and the report-generation pipeline are buildable well under
  Rs 50,000 (Provisional estimate — no vendor quotes obtained). The full
  Rs 2–3L ceiling is barely touched, which matters because it leaves margin
  for the outreach/testing iteration this plan actually depends on.
- **Rs 1L/month is a small number of transactions.** At an assumed
  Rs 15,000–20,000 ticket (Provisional), Rs 1L/month needs **5–7 sales in a
  month** — a low absolute unit count that a targeted, well-written cold
  outbound motion can plausibly reach by month 3, even with a conservative
  cold-outbound close rate.
- **It structurally fits the charter's "asset does the selling, work done
  once" rule** — see compliance note below — in a way none of the other
  candidates beat while also matching the operator's actual skill.

### Compliance note — this is the load-bearing assumption

The charter bans "ongoing service work" and requires the asset to sell
itself once. A per-customer diagnostic report looks like consulting unless
it's built correctly. The structure that makes this compliant: the *pipeline*
(intake form, data ingestion, AI-agent-generated report, fix-priority
templates) is built once, in Phase 2. Each sale after that consumes only
intake (customer self-serves an upload/share) + agent processing + **under
60 minutes of operator QA** before async delivery — no calls, no bespoke
strategy sessions, no retainer relationship. If this discipline slips —
if any customer gets a call, a follow-up strategy session, or hands-on
implementation help — the venture has quietly become a consulting practice
and is out of charter. This is a real risk, not a formality, and PHASE-0-GATE
should hold the line on it explicitly.

## 5. Steelman against this pick

Written adversarially, as the strongest case that this is the wrong call.

1. **Cold B2B outbound to strangers, asking for account access, converts far
   worse in practice than the model assumes.** The 5–7 sales/month needed
   for Rs 1L is being treated as "a low absolute number, therefore
   achievable." That inverts the real difficulty: getting *any* number of
   D2C founders — busy, skeptical, and asked to hand a stranger read access
   to their Klaviyo/GA4/Shopify data — to pay upfront, sight-unseen, from a
   cold LinkedIn message or email, with zero case studies, zero reviews, and
   zero social proof beyond a LinkedIn bio, is a genuinely hard cold-start
   B2B motion. Realistic cold outbound reply rates run low single digits and
   close rates lower still. Needing 5–7 closed *sales* a month could easily
   require 200–400+ quality outbound touches a month to sustain — a volume
   that is hard to hit inside a 7-hour/week ceiling once outreach research,
   personalization, sending, replying, agent QA, and delivery are all
   counted, especially in the same weeks the pipeline itself is still being
   built.
2. **The compliance boundary is fragile under real sales pressure.** The
   entire case that this isn't "ongoing service work" rests on the operator
   never getting on a call and never doing bespoke follow-up. But a cold,
   trust-starved B2B buyer being asked to pay Rs 15–20k for a report from an
   unknown solo operator is exactly the buyer most likely to ask for "just a
   15-minute call first" before paying — and the commercial instinct of
   someone who just left a growth-director job will be to take that call to
   not lose the sale. If that happens even occasionally, the venture drifts
   into the exact synchronous-service model the charter forbids, and the
   whole "work done once" premise underneath this pick quietly stops being
   true.
3. **Rs 5L/month is very likely a hard ceiling, not just a slow ramp — the
   venture may top out around Rs 1–2L/month solo.** At the assumed ticket,
   Rs 5L/month needs 25–33 sales/month. Even QA time alone (60 min ×
   25–33) is 25–33 hours in a single month against a 7-hour/*week* (~28–30
   hour/month) total budget — before any outreach, delivery, or intake
   handling is counted. If the real ambition is Rs 5L/month, this model
   cannot get there solo without breaking the "no hires, no contractor on
   the critical path" rule, meaning the ceiling of this pick may be
   structurally below the operator's actual ambition even if month-3 is hit.
4. **Zero defensibility means any early traction invites direct copying.**
   Scored 3/10 on purpose: there is no technical or data moat. If the
   diagnostic-report offer starts working, it is trivially copyable by any
   of the dozens of existing Klaviyo/CRO agencies and freelance ex-marketers
   who already have case studies and reviews the operator doesn't have on
   day one — they can out-compete on trust signals alone.
5. **The next-best candidate (C, consumer digital product) is close enough
   in score (4.95 vs 6.25) that the gap is arguably inside the noise of
   Provisional, ungrounded scoring** — if outbound B2B conversion turns out
   to be as bad as point 1 argues, C's faster time-to-first-customer (paid
   ads can produce a sale in days, not weeks of relationship-building) could
   make it the better bet, even though its CAC-quality bar is harder to
   clear at scale.

None of this makes the pick wrong — it makes the pick a real bet with a
specific, monitorable failure mode (outbound conversion rate and call
requests), which is exactly what the kill number below is designed to catch
early rather than after two months of sunk time.

## 6. Kill number and kill date

**Kill number (Provisional — will be finalized with real unit economics in
`02-finance/`):** If, by the kill date below, the venture has not closed at
least **5 paid diagnostic reports within a single calendar month** (i.e.
monthly revenue run-rate has not reached Rs 1,00,000 at the assumed
Rs 15,000–20,000 ticket), **or** any customer interaction has required a live
call or bespoke follow-up more than once, kill Candidate A and return to
Phase 0 rather than extending the timeline.

**Kill date:** **19 November 2026** (end of month 3 from Day 0, 2026-08-19 —
the charter's absolute-latest revenue deadline).
